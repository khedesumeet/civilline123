Highway Geometric Design

Key elements controlling safety and comfort

📏

Sight Distance

Length of roadway visible to the driver. Critical for safe stopping, passing, and decision-making.

SSD = vt + v²/(2gf)
  • SSD: Stopping Sight Distance
  • PSD: Passing Sight Distance
  • ISD: Intersection Sight Distance
  • Design speed is the primary control
🔄

Horizontal Alignment

Circular curves with transition spirals. Superelevation counteracts centrifugal force.

e + f = V²/(127R)
  • e = superelevation rate (%)
  • f = side friction factor
  • R = radius of curve (m)
  • Spiral length for comfort: L_s = V³/(CR)
📐

Vertical Alignment

Grades and vertical curves (summit and valley) provide safe stopping sight and comfortable ride.

L = NS²/(√(2h₁) + √(2h₂))²
  • Summit curves: SSD governs length
  • Valley curves: Headlight sight distance
  • Comfort: L = NV²/395 (m/s² criteria)
  • Appearance: L = 30–60 m for flat terrain
↔️

Cross-Section Elements

Lane width, shoulders, medians, side slopes, and drainage features define the highway envelope.

  • Lane width: 3.5–3.75 m (standard)
  • Shoulder: 2.5 m paved (highways)
  • Median: 4.5–12 m (divided)
  • Side slope: 2:1 (fill), 3:1 (cut)
  • Camber: 2–3% for drainage
🛣️

Design Speed & Class

Design speed determines all geometric features. IRC and AASHTO classify roads by function and terrain.

  • NH/SH: 80–120 km/h
  • MDR: 60–80 km/h
  • ODR: 40–60 km/h
  • VR: 20–30 km/h
  • Terrain: Plain, Rolling, Mountainous, Steep
🌉

Intersection Design

At-grade and grade-separated intersections control conflict points and capacity.

  • Channelization: Guide traffic paths
  • Roundabouts: Reduce severe crashes
  • Signal warrants: MUTCD/IRC criteria
  • LOS: A (best) to F (worst)

Highway Engineering Notes

Quick revision for exams and field use

📘 Sight Distance — Key Points

  • SSD Formula (IRC): SSD = 0.278Vt + V²/(254f) where V in km/h, t = reaction time (2.5 s IRC, 2.0 s AASHTO), f = longitudinal friction (0.35–0.40).
  • PSD: Minimum 4 times SSD on two-lane highways. PSD = d₁ + d₂ + d₃ + d₄ (initial maneuver, opposite lane, clearance, opposing vehicle).
  • Headlight Sight Distance: For valley curves, h₁ = 0.75 m (headlight), β = 1° upward beam. L = NS²/(1.5 + 0.035S) when S < L.
  • Set-back Distance: m = R − (R − d)cos(α/2) for sight line obstruction at horizontal curves. α = 28.65S/R (degrees).

📗 Superelevation & Curves — Key Points

  • IRC Max e: 7% for plain/rolling, 10% for hilly terrain (not snow-bound). AASHTO max: 6–12% depending on climate.
  • Minimum Radius: R_min = V²/(127(e_max + f_max)). If R < R_min, speed must be restricted or curve realigned.
  • Transition Curve: Spiral (clothoid) provides gradual introduction of centrifugal force. Rate of change of centrifugal acceleration: C = 80/(75+V) ≤ 0.6 m/s³ (IRC).
  • Widening: Extra width on curves: W_e = n·l²/(2R) + V/(9.5√R) (IRC). Mechanical widening + psychological widening.
  • Grade Compensation: For curves on steep grades, reduce grade by 30+R (max 75/R)% to maintain traction. Not needed if grade < 4%.

📙 Pavement Design — Key Points

  • Flexible (IRC:37): Design based on cumulative standard axles (MSA) and subgrade CBR. Vertical strain at top of subgrade ≤ f(N). Rutting criterion: ε_z ≤ 2.8×10⁻² × N^(-0.357).
  • Rigid (IRC:58): Design based on flexural strength of concrete, modulus of subgrade reaction (k), and traffic. Westergaard stress equations for corner, edge, interior loading. Dowel bars for load transfer, tie bars for longitudinal joints.
  • ESAL: Equivalent Single Axle Load = W·(axle load/80 kN)⁴. Fourth power law: pavement damage ∝ (load)⁴. Legal axle load limit: 10.2 tonnes (India), 18 tonnes tandem.
  • CBR Test: Penetration resistance at 2.5 mm and 5.0 mm. CBR = (test load / standard load) × 100. Soaked CBR for design (4 days immersion).

📕 Traffic Engineering — Key Points

  • PCU: Passenger Car Unit converts mixed traffic to equivalent cars. Truck/bus ≈ 3.0, motorcycle ≈ 0.5, cycle ≈ 0.2, animal cart ≈ 1.5 (IRC values).
  • Capacity: Basic capacity ≈ 2000 PCU/h/lane (ideal). Design capacity ≈ 1000–1600 PCU/h/lane. Level of Service (LOS) A–F based on V/C ratio and speed.
  • Weaving: Weaving length ≥ weaving width. Capacity of rotary: Q_w = (280w(1+e/w)(1-p/3))/(1+w/l) vehicles/hour (IRC formula).
  • Signal Design: Webster's optimum cycle: C₀ = (1.5L + 5)/(1 − Y) where Y = y₁ + y₂ + ... (sum of critical flow ratios). Lost time per phase ≈ 2–4 s.

Pavement Types

Structural systems for road construction

🛣️

Flexible

Asphalt layers on granular base. Distributes load through layered system.

🧱

Rigid

Concrete slab on subbase. High flexural strength, load via bending.

⚪

Composite

Concrete base + asphalt overlay. Combines benefits of both.

🟤

Semi-Rigid

Cement-stabilized base under asphalt. Intermediate stiffness.

♻️

Recycled

RAP (Reclaimed Asphalt Pavement) in new mixes. Sustainable.

🟢

Permeable

Porous asphalt/concrete for stormwater management.

Traffic & Intersection Design

Controlling flow and conflict points

🚦

Signalized Intersections

Fixed-time or vehicle-actuated signals control conflicting traffic streams. Design involves phase plan, cycle length, and green time allocation.

g_i = (y_i/Y)(C − L) + l_i − a_i
  • Webster's C₀ = (1.5L + 5)/(1 − Y)
  • Amber time: 2–5 s (function of approach speed)
  • All-red clearance: 1–3 s
⭕

Roundabouts

Circular intersections with yield control on entries. Reduce severe crashes by eliminating crossing conflicts.

  • IRC recommended: 10–30 m entry radius
  • Central island: 4–30 m radius
  • Weaving width: 7–18 m
  • Capacity: 3000–5000 PCU/hour
🚧

Channelization

Traffic islands and markings guide vehicle paths, reduce conflict area, and control speed at intersections.

  • Divisional islands separate opposing flows
  • Directional islands guide turning movements
  • Pedestrian refuge islands improve safety
  • Splitter islands at roundabouts

Formula Quick Reference

Essential highway engineering equations

📐 Geometric Design

Stopping Sight Distance

SSD = 0.278Vt + V²/(254f)

V in km/h, t = 2.5 s, f = 0.35–0.40

Superelevation

e + f = V²/(127R)

e in decimal, V in km/h, R in meters

Transition Spiral Length

L_s = V³/(CR) = 0.0215V³/(CR)

C = rate of change of acceleration (m/s³)

Valley Curve (Comfort)

L = NV²/395

N = grade change (%), V in km/h

🛣️ Pavement & Traffic

ESAL Factor

F = (L/80)⁴

L = axle load in kN; 80 kN = standard axle

Rotary Capacity (IRC)

Q_w = 280w(1+e/w)(1−p/3)/(1+w/l)

w = weaving width, l = length, p = proportion

Webster's Optimum Cycle

C₀ = (1.5L + 5)/(1 − Y)

L = total lost time, Y = sum y_i

Curve Widening (IRC)

W_e = nl²/(2R) + V/(9.5√R)

n = lanes, l = wheelbase (6 m), R = radius
