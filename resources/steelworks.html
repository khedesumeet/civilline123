Steel Structure Types

Primary applications of structural steel in civil engineering

🏢

Multi-Story Buildings

Steel moment-resisting frames and braced frames for commercial and residential high-rises. Offers rapid construction, large open spans, and seismic resilience.

  • Moment frames & braced frames
  • Composite floor systems
  • Lightweight vs. heavy steel
🌉

Steel Bridges

Truss, arch, cable-stayed, and suspension bridges utilizing high-strength steel. Superior strength-to-weight ratio enables longer spans.

  • Girder & truss bridges
  • Cable-stayed systems
  • Orthotropic steel decks
🏭

Industrial Structures

Factories, warehouses, power plants, and processing facilities requiring heavy loads, large clear spans, and crane support systems.

  • Portal frame sheds
  • Crane runway girders
  • Pipe racks & platforms
🗼

Towers & Masts

Communication towers, transmission pylons, and wind turbine towers using lattice or tubular steel configurations.

  • Self-supporting towers
  • Guyed masts
  • Monopole structures
🏟️

Space Frames & Roofs

Three-dimensional truss systems creating large column-free spans for airports, stadiums, and exhibition halls.

  • Space truss systems
  • Retractable roofs
  • Tensile membrane supports
🚧

Offshore & Marine

Jack-up rigs, platforms, and ship structures designed for extreme environmental loads and corrosive conditions.

  • Jacket platforms
  • Jack-up leg structures
  • Module support frames

Steel Connections

Methods of joining structural steel members

🔩

Bolted Connections

Most common site connection method using high-strength friction grip (HSFG) or bearing bolts. Easy to inspect, replace, and requires no skilled labor.

  • Shear connections (simple)
  • Moment connections (rigid)
  • HSFG vs. Black bolts
  • Pre-tensioned installation
⚡

Welded Connections

Shop and field welding using SMAW, GMAW, FCAW processes. Creates continuous rigid joints with full strength restoration.

  • Fillet welds (lap joints)
  • Butt/groove welds (full penetration)
  • Plug & slot welds
  • Weld quality grades (A-D)
🔗

Riveted & Pins

Historical connection method still found in heritage structures. Pins used for hinged connections in truss bridges and large assemblies.

  • Hot-driven rivets
  • Countersunk & button head
  • Pin connections (hinges)
  • Turnbuckles & adjusters

Design Codes & Standards

International standards governing structural steel design

AISC 360

USA - LRFD & ASD

Specification for Structural Steel Buildings. Covers design of members, connections, and seismic provisions using Load & Resistance Factor Design.

Eurocode 3 (EN 1993)

Europe - EC3

Design of steel structures including general rules, joint design, fatigue, and fire resistance. National annexes adapt for local conditions.

IS 800

India - LSM / WSM

Indian Standard for general construction in steel using Limit State Method. Covers plastic design, connections, and seismic detailing.

AS 4100

Australia

Steel structures standard using limit states design. Includes member capacity, connection design, and fire engineering provisions.

CSA S16

Canada

Design of steel structures using limit states design. Covers beams, columns, tension members, and composite construction.

BS 5950 / BS EN 1993

United Kingdom

BS 5950 (superseded) and current Eurocode 3 implementation for UK construction with UK National Annex provisions.
