Core Principles

Fundamentals of prestressed concrete behavior and design

⚡

Pre-tensioning vs Post-tensioning

Pre-tensioning: strands tensioned before concrete casting, force transferred by bond at release. Used in precast factories for beams, hollow-core slabs, piles.

  • Pre-tension: Factory production, bonded tendons
  • Post-tension: Cast first, tension later, ducts grouted or unbonded
  • Post-tension allows curved profiles and stage stressing
🔗

Prestressing Steel

High-strength steel essential to overcome relaxation and keep losses manageable. Typical ultimate strength 1860 MPa for strands.

  • Wires: 3–7 mm, cold drawn, f_pu ≈ 1470–1770 MPa
  • Strands: 7-wire, 12.7–15.7 mm, f_pu ≈ 1860 MPa
  • Bars: 20–40 mm, threaded, f_pu ≈ 1030 MPa
  • Modulus E_p ≈ 195 GPa (slightly lower than structural steel)
📉

Prestress Losses

Actual prestress at service is less than jacking force due to multiple loss mechanisms. Total losses typically 15–25% of initial force.

  • Immediate: Elastic shortening, friction/wobble, anchorage slip
  • Time-dependent: Shrinkage, creep, steel relaxation
  • Post-tension: friction + draw-in are major immediate losses
  • Pre-tension: elastic shortening + relaxation dominate
🏗️

Transfer vs Service Stages

Two critical design stages: at transfer (release/jacking) concrete is young and weak; at service, long-term losses and full loads apply.

  • Transfer: Check compression limit + tension limit; camber
  • Service: Decompression check, crack width, deflection
  • Transfer often governs top fiber stress (high P, low M)
  • Service often governs bottom fiber stress (low P, high M)
〰️

Cable Profiles & Equivalent Loads

The prestressing tendon exerts an equivalent upward load on the member equal to P·d²y/dx². Parabolic profiles counteract gravity loads.

w_eq = 8·P·e / L² (parabolic, simple span)
  • Straight: Constant eccentricity, end blocks needed
  • Parabolic: Ideal for uniform load balancing
  • Harped: Straight segments with kinks at supports
  • C.G.S. (cable) vs C.G.C. (concrete) define eccentricity e
🧱

End Block Design

The zone behind the anchorage where concentrated prestress disperses into linear distribution (St. Venant's principle ≈ beam depth).

  • Bursting: Transverse tension behind anchorage
  • Spalling: Surface tension at end face
  • Equilibrium: Eccentricity-induced moments
  • Reinforcement: helical/spiral + orthogonal mesh

📘 Prestress Losses — Detailed Breakdown

  • Elastic Shortening (ES): Concrete compresses under P, strand shortens with it. Pre-tension: Δf = m·f_cgp where m = E_p/E_c and f_cgp = stress at strand level. Post-tension: average loss = 0.5·m·f_cgp (sequential tensioning) or m·f_cgp (simultaneous).
  • Friction & Wobble (FR): P_x = P_jack·e^(−(μ·α + k·x)). μ = curvature friction coeff (0.15–0.55), k = wobble coeff (0.001–0.005 rad/m), α = cumulative angle change. Critical for long post-tensioned spans with curved profiles.
  • Anchorage Slip (AN): ΔP = √(Δ·E_p·A_p·p/L) where Δ = slip (2–8 mm), p = tendon force per unit length change. Causes force drop localized near the anchorage; affects short tendons more than long ones.
  • Shrinkage (SH): ε_sh = 200–400 × 10⁻⁶ for normal conditions. Loss = ε_sh·E_p. Higher in thin sections, low humidity, high water-cement ratio.
  • Creep (CR): Loss = E_p·φ·f_cgp/E_c where φ = creep coefficient (1.5–4.0). Depends on loading age, section thickness, and ambient humidity. Reduced by steam curing and high-strength concrete.
  • Relaxation (RE): Time-dependent loss of stress in steel under constant strain. Class 1 (ordinary): 8% at 1000h; Class 2 (low relaxation): 2.5% at 1000h. Increases with higher initial stress ratio (f_pi/f_pu).

📗 Section Analysis at Transfer & Service — Key Points

  • Stress Equations (Linear Elastic): f_top/bot = P/A ± P·e/Z ± M/Z. Sign convention: compression negative, tension positive (or vice versa — be consistent!). At transfer, M is usually self-weight only; at service, M includes dead + live + other imposed loads.
  • Allowable Stresses (Typical): Transfer compression ≤ 0.45 f_ci (or 0.6 f_ci depending on code). Transfer tension ≤ 0.25√f_ci (bonded) or zero (unbonded). Service compression ≤ 0.45 f_ck. Service tension ≤ 0 (Class 1), limited tension (Class 2), or full crack control (Class 3).
  • Kern Limits: If pressure line (thrust line) stays within middle third, no tension occurs. Upper kern = k_t = Z_b/A; Lower kern = k_b = Z_t/A. Maximum eccentricity without tension: e ≤ k + M/P.
  • Magnel Diagram: Graphical plot of 1/P vs e showing feasible zone satisfying all stress limits at transfer and service. Used to select P and e economically.

📙 Serviceability & Ultimate Limit States — Key Points

  • Deflection Control: Prestress causes upward camber. Long-term camber can grow due to creep and exceed upward limits. Downward deflection under live load must also be checked. Net deflection = load deflection − camber. Long-term multiplier ≈ 2.0–3.0 for sustained loads.
  • Ultimate Moment Capacity: Similar to RC but with effective prestress strain ε_pe added to concrete strain. Neutral axis depth and ultimate moment depend on whether section is under- or over-reinforced. For bonded tendons: M_u = A_p·f_ps·(d − 0.42x_u). f_ps depends on strain compatibility.
  • Shear Design: Prestress increases shear capacity by introducing compressive stress and inclined tendon force. V_c is enhanced by factor (1 + f_cp/f_ck) or similar code expressions. Web shear cracking (V_cw) vs flexure-shear cracking (V_ci) must both be checked.
  • Fatigue: Prestressing strands are vulnerable to fatigue under cyclic loading (e.g., railway bridges). Stress range in strands under service loads must be limited (typically ≤ 80 MPa for 2 million cycles).

📕 End Block & Anchorage Zone — Key Points

  • Bursting Force: T_transverse = 0.3P(1 − y_0/y_po) per Guyon, where y_0 = anchorage dimension, y_po = section half-depth. Bursting reinforcement designed at steel stress ≤ 200 MPa to control crack width.
  • Spalling: Surface reinforcement needed to prevent concrete spalling at the loaded face due to Poisson effect and local bearing. Bearing plate size must satisfy bearing stress ≤ 0.48 f_ci √(A₂/A₁) (ACI strut-and-tie check).
  • Strut-and-Tie Model: STM is the modern approach for disturbed regions (D-regions) like end blocks. Compressive struts fan out from anchorage; ties resist bursting and equilibrium forces. Angle between strut and tie should be ≥ 25°.
  • Multi-Anchorages: For wide beams with multiple anchors, each anchor has its own local end block. Global equilibrium must also be checked for the full end zone. Symmetric anchorages reduce equilibrium problems.

Prestressing Systems

Methods and applications in civil engineering

🏭

Pre-tensioning

Factory-based. Strands tensioned against bulkheads, concrete cast, cured, then released. Bonded by adhesion and mechanical wedge action.

🔧

Post-tensioning (Bonded)

Ducts formed in concrete, tendons threaded and stressed after curing. Ducts grouted for corrosion protection and bond.

⭕

Post-tensioning (Unbonded)

Tendons coated with grease and sheathed in plastic. Stressed after curing but NOT grouted. Used in slabs, replaceable.

🌉

External Prestressing

Tendons outside concrete section, deviated through diaphragms. Used in bridge strengthening and segmental construction.

📡

Dynamic/Smart Tendons

Equipped with sensors for force monitoring. Used in critical infrastructure for real-time health assessment.

🏗️

Segmental Construction

Match-cast segments joined with epoxy and post-tensioned. Balanced cantilever and span-by-span erection methods.

Formula Quick Reference

Essential equations for prestressed concrete design

📐 Section Stresses (Linear Elastic)

Top Fiber Stress

f_top = P/A − P·e/Z_t + M/Z_t

Compression negative. Check against allowable at transfer and service.

Bottom Fiber Stress

f_bot = P/A + P·e/Z_b − M/Z_b

Prestress causes compression at bottom; external moment causes tension.

Kern Limits

k_t = Z_b/A    k_b = Z_t/A

If pressure line lies within kern, no tension in extreme fibers.

Equivalent Upward Load

w_eq = 8·P·e / L²

Parabolic tendon profile balancing uniform gravity load.

🔧 Prestress Losses

Friction Loss

P_x = P_jack · e^(−(μα + kx))

μ = curvature coeff, k = wobble, α = angle change, x = distance

Elastic Shortening (Pre-tension)

Δf_p = m · f_cgp    m = E_p/E_c

f_cgp = concrete stress at tendon centroid after release

Shrinkage Loss

Δf_p = ε_sh · E_p

ε_sh ≈ 200–400 × 10⁻⁶ for normal weight concrete

Creep Loss

Δf_p = E_p · φ · f_cgp / E_c

φ = creep coefficient (1.5–4.0 depending on environment)

🏗️ Capacity & Design

Ultimate Moment (Bonded)

M_u = A_p · f_ps · (d − 0.42x_u)

f_ps from strain compatibility; x_u = neutral axis depth

Shear Capacity (Enhanced)

V_c = V_c0 · (1 + f_cp / f_ck)

f_cp = compressive stress from prestress at centroidal axis

Bursting Force (Guyon)

T_burst = 0.3P(1 − y₀/y_po)

Resisted by closed links in end block over 0.1y to y from end

Deflection (Parabolic Tendon)

δ_camber = 5·P·e·L² / (48·E_c·I)

Upward deflection due to prestress; subtract from load deflection
