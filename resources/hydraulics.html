Core Principles

Fundamental laws governing fluid behavior in civil engineering systems

💧

Continuity Equation

Conservation of mass for incompressible flow. The product of cross-sectional area and velocity remains constant along a streamline.

A₁V₁ = A₂V₂ = Q
  • Q = Flow rate (m³/s)
  • A = Cross-sectional area (m²)
  • V = Mean velocity (m/s)
📉

Bernoulli's Equation

Conservation of energy for ideal fluid flow. Total head (elevation + pressure + velocity) remains constant along a streamline.

z + p/γ + V²/2g = constant
  • z = Elevation head (m)
  • p/γ = Pressure head (m)
  • V²/2g = Velocity head (m)
⚡

Energy Equation (Real Fluids)

Modified Bernoulli including head losses due to friction (major losses) and fittings (minor losses).

z₁ + p₁/γ + V₁²/2g = z₂ + p₂/γ + V₂²/2g + hL
  • hL = hf (friction) + hm (minor)
  • Major: Darcy-Weisbach equation
  • Minor: K × V²/2g or equivalent length
🌊

Momentum Equation

Newton's second law applied to fluid control volumes. Essential for calculating forces on bends, nozzles, and hydraulic structures.

ΣF = ρQ(V₂ - V₁)
  • ΣF = Sum of external forces (N)
  • ρ = Fluid density (kg/m³)
  • Used for jump analysis, thrust blocks
🔬

Reynolds Number

Dimensionless number predicting flow regime. Critical for selecting appropriate friction factor and flow equations.

Re = ρVD/μ = VD/ν
  • Re < 2000: Laminar flow
  • Re > 4000: Turbulent flow
  • 2000–4000: Transitional zone
🌀

Froude Number

Ratio of inertial to gravitational forces. Determines flow regime in open channels and hydraulic jump type.

Fr = V/√(gD)
  • Fr < 1: Subcritical (tranquil)
  • Fr = 1: Critical flow
  • Fr > 1: Supercritical (rapid)

Civil Engineering Hydraulics Notes

Key study material for exams and field reference

📘 Open Channel Flow — Key Points

  • Manning's Equation is the most widely used formula for uniform open channel flow in civil engineering: V = (1/n) × R^(2/3) × S^(1/2)
  • Hydraulic Radius (R) = A/P, where A = flow area and P = wetted perimeter. For wide rectangular channels, R ≈ y (flow depth).
  • Normal Depth (yₙ) occurs when gravitational force exactly balances friction force — water surface is parallel to channel bed.
  • Critical Depth (yc) occurs when specific energy is minimum for a given discharge: yc = (q²/g)^(1/3) for rectangular channels.
  • Specific Energy E = y + V²/2g. For a given E, two possible depths exist (alternate depths) except at critical depth.
  • Channel slope classifications: Mild (S < Sc), Critical (S = Sc), Steep (S > Sc), Horizontal (S = 0), Adverse (S < 0).

📗 Pipe Flow & Water Supply — Key Points

  • Darcy-Weisbach: hf = f × (L/D) × (V²/2g). The friction factor f depends on Re and relative roughness (ε/D) — use Moody diagram or Colebrook-White equation.
  • Hazen-Williams is empirical and only for water in turbulent flow: V = 0.85 × C × R^0.63 × S^0.54 (metric). C ranges from 90 (old pipes) to 150 (new PVC).
  • Minor losses in pipe fittings: hm = K × V²/2g. Common K values: sharp entrance (0.5), 90° elbow (0.9), gate valve open (0.2), sudden expansion (1.0).
  • Equivalent Pipe Method: For pipes in series, hf = hf1 + hf2 + ... For parallel pipes, head loss is same in each branch; total Q = Q₁ + Q₂ + ...
  • Water Hammer is pressure surge caused by sudden valve closure. Wave speed a = √(K/ρ) / √(1 + (K×D)/(E×t)). Use surge tanks, air chambers, or slow-closing valves for mitigation.

📙 Hydraulic Structures — Key Points

  • Hydraulic Jump occurs when supercritical flow transitions to subcritical. Energy is dissipated as turbulence and heat. Used downstream of spillways and sluice gates for energy dissipation.
  • Jump Equation (Belanger): y₂/y₁ = 0.5 × [√(1 + 8Fr₁²) - 1]. Length of jump ≈ 6–7 × y₂.
  • Weirs: Rectangular sharp-crested: Q = (2/3) × Cd × √(2g) × L × H^(3/2). Triangular (V-notch): Q = (8/15) × Cd × √(2g) × tan(θ/2) × H^(5/2).
  • Spillways: Ogee crest profile follows the trajectory of underside of nappe from sharp-crested weir. Design head Hd must match actual head for optimal pressure distribution.
  • Culverts: Flow controlled by inlet (headwater depth) or outlet (tailwater + barrel friction). Inlet control: use nomographs or FHWA equations. Outlet control: use energy equation through barrel.

📕 Groundwater & Seepage — Key Points

  • Darcy's Law: v = k × i, where k = coefficient of permeability, i = hydraulic gradient (Δh/L). Valid for laminar flow in soils (Re < 1–10).
  • Laplace Equation governs 2D seepage: ∂²h/∂x² + ∂²h/∂y² = 0. Solved using flow nets: orthogonal network of equipotential lines and flow lines.
  • Flow Net Properties: Nf = number of flow channels, Nd = number of equipotential drops. Seepage q = k × H × (Nf/Nd) per unit width.
  • Piping/Quicksand occurs when upward seepage force equals submerged weight of soil: critical hydraulic gradient ic = (G - 1)/(1 + e), where G = specific gravity, e = void ratio.
  • Well Hydraulics: For confined aquifer (Theis): s = (Q/4πT) × W(u). For steady-state confined (Thiem): Q = 2πT(s₁ - s₂) / ln(r₂/r₁).

Hydraulic Structures

Civil engineering structures for water control, conveyance, and measurement

🌊 Dams & Reservoirs
Gravity, arch, and buttress dams for water storage, flood control, and hydroelectric power. Key design considerations: stability, seepage, spillway capacity, and foundation treatment.
  • Gravity: concrete mass stability
  • Arch: thin concrete, canyon sites
  • Embankment: earth/rockfill
🏗️ Weirs & Barrages
Low-head structures for flow measurement, diversion, and raising water levels. Sharp-crested for measurement; broad-crested for flow control.
  • Rectangular, Cippoletti, V-notch
  • Broad-crested: critical flow control
  • Submerged vs. free flow conditions
🚇 Culverts & Cross-Drainage
Closed conduits under embankments for water passage. Design considers inlet control, outlet control, and headwater elevation constraints.
  • Box, circular, pipe-arch shapes
  • Inlet/outlet control nomographs
  • HW/D criteria for road overtopping
⚡ Spillways & Energy Dissipators
Safely pass flood flows downstream. Energy dissipation prevents scour at toe. Types include ogee, side channel, shaft, siphon, and labyrinth.
  • Ogee crest profile design
  • Hydraulic jump stilling basin
  • Flip bucket / ski-jump buckets
🌾 Canals & Channels
Open channels for irrigation, water supply, and drainage. Design involves alignment, section geometry, lining, and regulation structures.
  • Lined vs. unlined canals
  • Trapezoidal, rectangular, circular
  • Canal falls, cross-regulators
🕳️ Wells & Pumps
Groundwater extraction systems. Design includes aquifer type, well screen, pump capacity, and drawdown constraints.
  • Confined vs. unconfined aquifers
  • Radial collector wells
  • Pump characteristic curves

Formula Quick Reference

Essential equations for hydraulic calculations

📐 Open Channel Flow

Manning's Equation (Metric)

V = (1/n) R^(2/3) S^(1/2)
Q = (1/n) A R^(2/3) S^(1/2)

V=velocity, n=roughness, R=hydraulic radius, S=slope

Chezy Formula

V = C √(RS)

C = Chezy coefficient; C = (1/n) R^(1/6) (Manning relation)

Critical Depth (Rectangular)

yc = (q²/g)^(1/3)

q = Q/b (discharge per unit width), g = 9.81 m/s²

Specific Energy

E = y + V²/2g = y + Q²/(2gA²)

Minimum E occurs at critical depth

🔧 Pipe Flow

Darcy-Weisbach

hf = f (L/D) (V²/2g)

f = friction factor from Moody diagram or Colebrook-White

Hazen-Williams (Metric)

V = 0.85 C R^0.63 S^0.54

C = 130–150 for new pipes, 90–100 for old cast iron

Minor Losses

hm = K V²/2g = f (Le/D) V²/2g

K = loss coefficient, Le = equivalent length

Colebrook-White

1/√f = -2 log[ε/3.7D + 2.51/Re√f]

Implicit equation; solved iteratively or via Haaland approximation

🏗️ Structures & Measurement

Rectangular Weir (Francis)

Q = 1.84 (L - 0.1nH) H^(3/2)

n = number of end contractions, L = crest length (m)

Triangular Weir (Thomson)

Q = 1.42 H^(5/2)

For 90° V-notch; most accurate for H = 0.05–0.38 m

Hydraulic Jump Conjugate Depth

y₂ = (y₁/2)[√(1+8Fr₁²) - 1]

Energy loss: ΔE = (y₂ - y₁)³ / (4y₁y₂)

Orifice Flow

Q = Cd A √(2gH)

Cd ≈ 0.61–0.65 for sharp-edged orifice
