What is Pile Engineering?

Deep foundations when surface ground isn't strong enough

🎯 Purpose

Piles transfer building loads through soft or waterlogged soils to deeper competent strata like dense sand, stiff clay, or bedrock via end bearing and skin friction.

Foundation

⚠️ When Needed

Required for low-bearing surface soils, heavy concentrated loads, waterfront scour, uplift resistance, lateral loads, or frost depth transfer.

Geotechnical

📐 Key Parameters

Critical: pile diameter, embedded length, soil shear strength (c, φ), unit weight (γ), groundwater depth, material properties, and load combinations.

Design

🏗️ Load Transfer

Ultimate capacity Qu = Qb (end bearing) + Qs (skin friction). Working load Qa = Qu / Factor of Safety (typically 2.0–3.0).

Mechanics
Qu = Qb + Qs
Ultimate = End Bearing + Skin Friction

Types of Piles

Different types for different ground conditions

🏗️

Driven Precast Concrete

Reinforced or prestressed concrete piles driven with impact hammers. High capacity and durability.

⌀ 250–600 mm 6–20 m 500–3000 kN
🔩

Driven Steel (H-Pile / Pipe)

Steel H-sections or pipes driven to refusal. Excellent for dense layers. Can be spliced.

H-section / Pipe 10–40 m 800–5000 kN
🪵

Timber Piles

Treated tree trunks for light loads. Historically common; now limited to marine use.

⌀ 200–400 mm 6–18 m 100–400 kN
🌀

Bored Cast-In-Situ

Holes drilled with augers, filled with reinforced concrete. Minimal vibration.

⌀ 300–2000 mm 10–60 m 1000–15000 kN
🔄

CFA (Continuous Flight Auger)

Hollow auger drills while concrete pumps through the stem. Fast and vibration-free.

⌀ 300–1200 mm 15–30 m 500–4000 kN
🔧

Micropiles

Small-diameter drilled piles (⌀ 100–300 mm) with grout and steel. For underpinning and retrofitting.

⌀ 100–300 mm 5–30 m 100–1000 kN
🌀

Helical (Screw) Piles

Steel shafts with helical plates screwed into ground. Immediate capacity, no spoils.

⌀ 50–300 mm 3–15 m 50–500 kN
⚡

Composite Piles

Concrete-filled steel tubes or FRP piles. Best of multiple materials for corrosion resistance.

Hybrid Variable High durability

Installation Process

From site investigation to pile cutoff

1

Site Investigation

Boreholes, SPT/CPT tests determine soil stratification, strength, and groundwater. Lab testing provides c, φ, and unit weights.

2

Pile Design

Calculate length, diameter, and spacing from structural loads and soil capacity. Analyze group effects and design pile caps.

3

Driving / Boring

Driven: hydraulic hammers or vibratory drivers. Bored: temporary casing or bentonite slurry. CFA: continuous auger withdrawal.

4

Quality Control

Monitor driving resistance, concrete volume, verticality. Post-install: static/dynamic load tests and integrity testing.

5

Pile Cap Construction

Cut pile tops to design level, expose reinforcement, tie into pile cap. Cap distributes column loads to the pile group.

Bearing Capacity

How much load can a single pile carry?

📊 End Bearing (Qb)

Resistance at pile tip. Sand: Qb = Ab × σ'v × Nq. Clay: Qb = Ab × c × Nc. Ab = base area, Nq/Nc = bearing factors.

Tip Resistance

🔗 Skin Friction (Qs)

Shaft-soil shear resistance. Sand: Qs = As × Ks × σ'v × tan(δ). Clay: Qs = As × α × cu. α = adhesion factor (0.3–1.0).

Shaft Resistance

🛡️ Factor of Safety

Qa = Qu / FS. FS = 2.0–2.5 with load testing. FS = 2.5–3.0 from soil parameters alone without pile-specific tests.

Safety

📉 Negative Friction

When soil settles more than the pile, downdrag develops along the shaft. Must be added to structural loads in design.

Downdrag
Qa = (Ab·qb + As·fs) / F.S.
Allowable = (End Bearing + Skin Friction) / F.S.

Pile Testing

Verifying installed piles meet design requirements

⚖️ Static Load Test

Gold standard. Incremental loads applied via kentledge. Measures settlement until failure or 2× working load. Most reliable but slow (2–3 days).

Static

🔨 Dynamic Load Test (PDA)

Strain gauges + accelerometers at pile head. Falling weight strikes pile; CAPWAP wave analysis calculates capacity. 8–10 piles/day.

Dynamic

📡 High-Strain Testing

Heavier drop weights (up to 40t) for large bored piles. Assesses shaft resistance, end bearing, and integrity in one test.

Dynamic

🔊 Low-Strain (PIT)

Hand hammer impacts pile head. Reflected waves reveal necking, cracks, or inclusions. Quick, non-destructive screening of 100% piles.

Integrity

🎵 Sonic Integrity

Access tubes cast into pile. Ultrasonic pulses create 3D concrete quality profile. Highly accurate for defect detection.

Integrity

🧪 Core Extraction

Diamond core drilled and tested for compressive strength. Direct concrete verification. Destructive; limited sample size.

Destructive
