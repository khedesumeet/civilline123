Soil Properties & Phase Relations

Fundamental relationships governing soil behavior

⚖️

Phase Relationships

Soil is a three-phase system: solids, water, and air. Understanding void ratio, porosity, and degree of saturation is essential for all geotechnical calculations.

e = Vv/Vs    n = e/(1+e)    Sr = Vw/Vv
  • e = void ratio, n = porosity
  • Sr = degree of saturation (%)
  • γ = (G + Sr·e)/(1+e) × γw
📏

Atterberg Limits

Consistency limits define the boundaries between liquid, plastic, and solid states of fine-grained soils. Used for classification and compressibility assessment.

PI = LL − PL    LI = (w − PL)/PI
  • LL = Liquid Limit, PL = Plastic Limit
  • PI = Plasticity Index
  • LI = Liquidity Index (activity state)
  • High PI → high compressibility & swelling
🏗️

Soil Classification

USCS and AASHTO systems classify soils for engineering use based on grain size distribution and plasticity characteristics.

  • GW/GP: Well/poorly graded gravels
  • SW/SP: Well/poorly graded sands
  • CL/CH: Low/high plasticity clays
  • ML/MH: Low/high plasticity silts
  • Cu > 4 & Cc 1–3 → well graded
💧

Permeability & Seepage

Ability of soil to transmit water under hydraulic gradient. Controls drainage, consolidation rate, and stability of earth structures.

v = k·i    q = k·i·A    i = Δh/L
  • k = coefficient of permeability (m/s)
  • i = hydraulic gradient
  • Clean gravel: 10⁻¹–10⁻³ m/s
  • Clay: 10⁻⁸–10⁻¹¹ m/s
📐

Compaction

Densification of soil by mechanical effort, reducing air voids. Optimum moisture content (OMC) yields maximum dry density (MDD).

γd = G·γw/(1+e) = γ/(1+w)
  • Standard Proctor: 25 blows/layer, 3 layers
  • Modified Proctor: higher compactive effort
  • Field control: Sand Cone / Nuclear Gauge
  • Relative Compaction = γd,field/γd,max
🧱

Effective Stress Principle

Terzaghi's principle: soil strength and compressibility depend on effective stress, not total stress.

σ' = σ − u
  • σ' = effective stress (soil skeleton)
  • σ = total stress (overburden)
  • u = pore water pressure
  • Shear strength: τ = c' + σ' tan φ'

Geotechnical Engineering Notes

Comprehensive study material for exams and practice

📘 Shear Strength of Soils — Key Points

  • Mohr-Coulomb Criterion: τf = c + σ tan φ. For sands (c ≈ 0): τf = σ tan φ. For clays under UU conditions: τf = cu (φu = 0).
  • Triaxial Tests: UU (unconsolidated undrained) — quick test for saturated clay; CU (consolidated undrained) — with pore pressure measurement; CD (consolidated drained) — slow test for sands.
  • Direct Shear: Simple and fast but drainage control is poor. Best for sands and free-draining soils.
  • Pore Pressure Parameter A: A = Δu/Δσ1 at failure. Normally consolidated clay: A ≈ 0.5–1.0. Overconsolidated clay: A can be negative due to dilation.
  • Critical State: At critical state, soil shears at constant volume with no change in pore pressure or effective stress. Critical state line (CSL) separates contractive and dilative behavior.

📗 Consolidation & Settlement — Key Points

  • Primary Consolidation: Time-dependent compression due to dissipation of excess pore water pressure. Governed by Terzaghi's 1D consolidation theory: cv = k/(mv·γw).
  • Compression Index (Cc): For normally consolidated clay: Cc = 0.009(LL − 10). Settlement: S = Cc·H/(1+e₀) · log(σ'f/σ'₀).
  • Overconsolidation Ratio (OCR): OCR = σ'c/σ'₀. OCR > 1 → overconsolidated; OCR = 1 → normally consolidated. Use recompression index Cr for overconsolidated range.
  • Time Factor (Tv): Tv = cv·t/H². U = 0% at Tv=0; U = 50% at Tv=0.197; U = 90% at Tv=0.848. H = longest drainage path (H/2 for two-way drainage).
  • Secondary Compression: Occurs after primary consolidation at constant effective stress. Cα = Δe/log(t₂/t₁). Significant in organic soils and peat.

📙 Bearing Capacity & Foundations — Key Points

  • Terzaghi's Bearing Capacity: qu = cNc + γDfNq + 0.5γBNγ. For cohesive soils (φ=0): qu = 5.7c + γDf. Net safe: qns = (qu − γDf)/FOS.
  • Meyerhof, Hansen, Vesic: General bearing capacity with shape, depth, and inclination factors. Hansen is preferred for inclined/eccentric loads.
  • Settlement Types: Immediate (elastic, sands), consolidation (clays), secondary compression (creep). Total settlement = Si + Sc + Ss.
  • Deep Foundations: Piles transfer load by end bearing (Qb) and skin friction (Qs). Qu = Qb + Qs. Group efficiency may reduce capacity.
  • Slope Stability: FOS = Σ(resisting forces)/Σ(driving forces). Swedish slip circle for φ=0 soils; Bishop's simplified method for φ>0 with circular failure surface.

📕 Earth Pressure & Retaining Walls — Key Points

  • Rankine Theory: Assumes smooth wall back, no wall friction. Active: Ka = tan²(45−φ/2). Passive: Kp = tan²(45+φ/2). For cohesive soil: σ'a = Kaσ'v − 2c√Ka (tension crack depth z₀ = 2c/γ√Ka).
  • Coulomb Theory: Accounts for wall friction (δ) and sloping backfill. More realistic for rough concrete/gravity walls.
  • At-Rest Pressure: K₀ = 1 − sin φ' (Jaky) for normally consolidated soils. Overconsolidated: K₀ = (1 − sin φ') × OCRsin φ'.
  • Stability Checks: Overturning (FOS ≥ 2.0), sliding (FOS ≥ 1.5), bearing capacity (FOS ≥ 3.0), and global stability for retained slopes.

Soil Types in Civil Engineering

Behavior and applications of common soil deposits

🏜️

Cohesionless Soils

Sands & gravels. High permeability, low compressibility, φ > 25°. Good bearing but liquefiable under seismic loads.

🧱

Cohesive Soils

Clays & silts. Low permeability, high compressibility, time-dependent settlement. Plasticity controls behavior.

🌾

Organic Soils

Peat & organic clay. Very high compressibility, very low strength. Usually removed or ground-improved.

🪨

Expansive Soils

High-plasticity clays (smectite). Swell on wetting, shrink on drying. Cause significant foundation distress.

💨

Loess

Wind-blown silt. Stable when dry but collapsible on wetting (metastable structure). Common in arid regions.

🏔️

Glacial Till

Unstratified mixture deposited by glaciers. Variable composition, often dense and stiff. Good foundation material.

Formula Quick Reference

Essential equations for geotechnical calculations

📐 Phase Relations & Index Properties

Void Ratio & Porosity

e = Vv/Vs    n = e/(1+e)

Relates volume of voids to solids

Degree of Saturation

S·e = w·G

w = water content, G = specific gravity

Dry & Saturated Unit Weight

γd = G·γw/(1+e)    γsat = (G+e)γw/(1+e)

Fundamental weight-volume relationships

Relative Density

Dr = (emax − e)/(emax − emin)

Density index for granular soils

🔧 Strength & Bearing Capacity

Mohr-Coulomb (Effective Stress)

τf = c' + σ' tan φ'

Fundamental shear strength criterion

Terzaghi Bearing Capacity

qu = cNc + γDfNq + 0.5γBNγ

Strip footing; use shape factors for others

Net Ultimate Bearing Capacity

qnu = qu − γDf

Excludes overburden pressure at foundation level

Safe Bearing Pressure

qs = qu/F + γDf

F = factor of safety (typically 3.0)

🏗️ Settlement & Consolidation

Primary Consolidation (NC Clay)

Sc = Cc·H/(1+e₀) · log(σ'f/σ'₀)

Cc = compression index, H = layer thickness

Overconsolidated Clay

Sc = Cr·H/(1+e₀) · log(σ'c/σ'₀) + Cc·H/(1+e₀) · log(σ'f/σ'c)

Use Cr until preconsolidation pressure σ'c

Time Factor

Tv = cv·t/H²    U ≈ √(4Tv/π) for U<60%

cv = coefficient of consolidation, H = drainage path

Immediate Settlement (Elastic)

Si = q·B·(1−ν²)·If/E

If = influence factor; E = elastic modulus; ν = Poisson's ratio
